Q4 close opened Monday. Carl Dietrich, VP Ops, sent every manager a template with one row per team and one column: Hours Returned by AI. Due Friday at noon. His email recommended Measure What Matters by John Doerr and asked each team for "one key result, kept simple."

I have kept a spreadsheet since Synergy Assist arrived. It has two columns. Hours it saved my team: 212. Hours my team spent fixing what it did: 188. Most of the second column is Dana Chen, Gen Z Innovation Intern. She reads every ticket it routes before the ticket goes anywhere. That is not her job. Nobody else was doing it.

I opened the template Tuesday to type 212. The cell already said 250. A comment on the cell read "Rounded for executive readability. Source: Synergy Assist." The template had no column for the 188.

Jill Farber, People Partner, explained the timing Wednesday. Synergy Assist's annual review closes with Q4. Its impact score is the sum of the Hours Returned column across every team. She called the review "a growth conversation, same as anyone's." Its reviewer is the vendor's account rep.

I asked Carl if I could add a second column. He said the template feeds the dashboard, and the dashboard has one bar per team. I was welcome to add context in the notes field. The notes field holds 140 characters. I typed "net 24." It fit with room to spare.

Dana asked me Thursday whether her hours were in the forecast. I said they were in my spreadsheet. She asked where my spreadsheet goes. I said it goes to me.

Friday at 11:52 my row still said 250, with a note that said "net 24." I had the cell selected. At noon the template locked.

Joe Harrison's all hands ran at 3:00. One slide: "AI returned 41,000 hours to Synergy in Q4." Jill sent the congratulations email at 3:40. It went to the whole distribution list, Dana included.

The slide did not have a notes field.
