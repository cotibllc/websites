Three more meetings appeared on my calendar this morning. Same pattern as April. Confident subject lines. Attendee lists assembled from whoever emailed about a related noun last week. No owner in the body. No decision named. The organizer field said Synergy Assist.

I did not accept them. I also did not decline them yet. Declining takes longer than it should, because declining means writing a reason that will be read by twelve people who also did not ask for the meeting, and silence looks like consent on a shared calendar.

I canceled last month's series after the second empty room. That took forty minutes of replies, a short thread with my manager, and one message to IT asking whether the tool could be told to stop. IT said the feature is working as designed. My manager said the dashboard shows collaboration volume up eleven percent.

The invites are real. The rooms fill with people who are polite enough to show up and honest enough to admit, around minute eight, that nobody knows what we are supposed to decide. Then someone volunteers to take it offline, which means opening the original email thread Synergy Assist summarized into a meeting.

Synergy Assist does not join. It never has. It creates the obligation, stamps the time, and leaves the attendance to whoever still has a badge that opens Zoom.

I asked, again, who owns a meeting the bot creates when the bot is not a person. HR said it is not an employee, so it cannot own anything. IT said it is a service account with calendar scopes, so it can own the invite object. Legal was not on the thread.

Thursday's meeting ran with nine of twelve people. Zero agenda items closed. The same organizer has already placed next week's follow-up on the calendar.

The three from this morning are still sitting there. I have not declined them.
