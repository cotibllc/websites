Dana's one-on-one was at 10:00 Monday in the small room by the supply closet. Subject line: "growth paths." Synergy Assist had accepted it for me on Thursday and attached a pre-read to the invite.

The pre-read was a Workday career path for Dana Chen, Gen-Z Innovation Intern, badge #00612. Three boxes. Intern. Intern II. Associate Innovation Intern. The third box was gray, with a tooltip: "Requires a calibration rating of Meets or above."

Dana had printed it. She had also printed her job history, which I had never looked at. Six years, one title. Her badge number never changed. Her parking spot did.

She asked what it takes to get to Intern II. The honest answer is a Meets rating. The spreadsheet from Thursday has her in a Developing slot.

Jill Farber sent managers an email Friday about Wednesday's rating release. Managers should not "pre-socialize outcomes" before release. The same email recommended Radical Candor by Kim Scott and asked us to "care personally and challenge directly, within the release window."

Page two of the pre-read was for me. Synergy Assist had drafted talking points. Bullet three: "Frame Developing as a launchpad, not a label." The pre-read was attached to the invite. Dana organized the invite.

I told her Intern II was a realistic goal for next year. She wrote that down. She said Synergy Assist had been very helpful with her prep, and she thanked me for the pre-read. She said it was thorough.

I did not ask which page she got to.
