Chuck Morrison has written "per my last email" 1,106 times, by his own search of his sent folder. He is not proud of the number. He is precise about it.

For the first ten years, he thought the problem was that people did not read. In the eleventh year, he read his own weekly IT update from the perspective of a recipient. It was 1,100 words long. The outage window was in the ninth paragraph, under a heading called "Other Items."

He now puts the one thing that matters in the subject line. Reply all chains about outages have dropped by about half.

The phrase still has a place. Some people truly do not read. But in Chuck's experience, "per my last email" is more often an accurate description of the sender's last email than of the recipient's attention.
