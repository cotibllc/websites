faqs:
  - question: "What does 'headcount neutral' mean?"
    answer: "That the total number of positions in an organization or team will be the same after a change as before it. It is a statement about a number, not about any individual. Chuck Morrison has documented that the people hearing it almost always interpret it as a statement about themselves."
  - question: "Can a headcount neutral reorg still eliminate jobs?"
    answer: "Yes. A role can be removed and a different role opened, and the count remains neutral. The person in the removed role is invited to apply for the new one, which often requires a skill set they were never asked to develop. Neutral is a property of the spreadsheet. It is not a property of the people in it."
  - question: "What questions should you ask when a change is announced as headcount neutral?"
    answer: "Ask whether any current roles are being removed, whether any new roles are being opened, and whether the people in the removed roles have first claim on the new ones. If the answer to the third question is 'we'll share more as details are finalized,' the details are already final."
  - question: "Is 'headcount neutral' ever fully honest?"
    answer: "It can be. When every person keeps a role and the reorg only changes reporting lines, the phrase is accurate and reassuring. Leaders who mean it that way usually say so directly: 'everyone keeps their job.' The more precise the reassurance, the more it can be trusted."

Chuck Morrison's department has been headcount neutral through four reorgs.

In that time it has contained 22 different people. Chuck is one of two who were present for all four.

The phrase is mathematically true in every case. The number on the slide has not moved. What moved was everything underneath the number: the titles, the reporting lines, the skills required, and the names. A department can stay the same size while becoming an entirely different department.

Leadership presents this as stability. The spreadsheet agrees. The people in the second row of the town hall, doing math on their own roles, are less sure.
