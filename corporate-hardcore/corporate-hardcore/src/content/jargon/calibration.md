Chuck Morrison was calibrated for the first time in his second year. His manager told him afterward that he had "done great work, the numbers just didn't work out this year."

He asked what the numbers were. His manager said that was a calibration question.

Chuck has since been in the room. The room has a slide. The slide has a bell curve. The bell curve has a shape that was approved by Finance in September. Everything after the slide is a negotiation over which names fit inside it.

He does not blame the managers. Most of them argue hard and lose quietly. He blames the order of operations. When the curve arrives before the people do, the meeting is not measuring performance. It is fitting performance to a container.

The container is always the same size. The work never is.
