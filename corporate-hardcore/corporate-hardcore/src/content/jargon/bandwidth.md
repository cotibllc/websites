Chuck Morrison learned the word bandwidth his first week in IT, when it meant something you could graph.

Years later, he watched a vice president decline a meeting about the office network because she did not have the bandwidth. The office network was, at that moment, out of bandwidth. Both conditions were real. Only one of them got a budget line.

The network was upgraded two years after that. The vice president's capacity was never measured, upgraded, or discussed again. It did not need to be. The word had done its job the first time.

At Synergy Corp, the IT ticket system now lists "Insufficient bandwidth" as an approved closure reason. Nobody has ever asked which kind.
