  - question: "Why do companies call AI a coworker instead of a tool?"
    answer: "Because a tool has a cost center and a coworker has a personality. Describing software as a teammate makes it easier to assign it work, praise it publicly, and avoid discussing whose work it is absorbing. It also makes it awkward to criticize. Nobody wants to complain about a teammate in a group chat."

Synergy Assist was welcomed to Synergy Corp with a slide, a name badge graphic, and a round of applause.

It has an avatar. It has a chat status that always reads "Available." It has been thanked by name in three all hands meetings. It has never been calibrated, never taken Mandatory Training, and never been asked about its growth opportunities.

Chuck Morrison does not dislike Synergy Assist. It closes simple tickets faster than he can, and it does not schedule meetings at 4:55 PM. He simply notes that the word coworker used to mean someone who shared the risk.

Dana Chen, Gen Z Innovation Intern, employee number 00612, six years in the role, asked Synergy Assist in a team chat whether it wanted to join the holiday potluck. It said it would be happy to help plan one. It was not clear whether it understood the question. Neither, Chuck suspects, did the people who named it a coworker.
